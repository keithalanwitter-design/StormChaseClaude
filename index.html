The window

  • Storms usually fire mid-to-late afternoon once the cap breaks.
  • Tornadoes peak about an hour before sunset (Brown et al. 2021).
  • After sunset the low-level jet can roughly double 0–1 km SRH within three hours (SPC).
  • Half of our Illinois–Missouri cases hit between November and March; don't put the gear away in fall.

After morning storms

  • Leftover outflow boundaries are prime targets; about 70% of VORTEX tornadoes were tied to one (NWS).
  • Recovery needs moisture as much as sunshine: watch dewpoints climb back and winds turn south.
  • Models bring the air back too fast; trust surface observations (NSSL).

Picking the storm

Run every candidate through these checks in order. A storm that fails one isn't the target.

  1. Is it surface-based?It should sit in the warm sector with dewpoints in the mid-60s. If not, drop south to the warm side of the boundary.
  2. Does it have clean inflow?Favor the tail-end or most isolated storm with nothing to its south or southeast. If not, switch to that storm.
  3. Is it rotating?Look for a tightening velocity couplet. If rotation is weak, stay ahead for two or three scans and drop it if outflow surges.
  4. Is it holding discrete?No merging, no bowing. If it is congealing, move to the next isolated cell south.
  5. Commit 3–5 miles southeast of the updraft baseKeep two escape routes, south and east, and re-check every scan or two.
